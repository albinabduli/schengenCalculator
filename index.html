How the rule is counted

As a citizen of North Macedonia you can stay in the Schengen area without a visa for 90 days in any 180-day period. The period is not a fixed half-year: for every single day of your stay, look back over the previous 180 days (that day plus the 179 before it) and count the days you were inside. That count must never exceed 90.

The "Fixed periods from first entry" option instead splits time into back-to-back 180-day blocks starting on your very first entry, each with its own 90 days. Many people find that easier to follow, but it is not how the rule is written: a stay that straddles two blocks can pass the fixed count and still fail the rolling one. The rolling count is always shown alongside so you can see both.

The day you enter and the day you leave both count as full days, even a late-night arrival or early-morning departure. Days between Schengen countries count once — this tracker merges overlapping or back-to-back trips automatically.

Days spent on a national long-stay visa or residence permit for a Schengen country are not counted against the 90 days. Since the EU Entry/Exit System started recording border crossings electronically, overstays are detected automatically, so keep your own log matching your passport stamps or EES record.
